The DSS Verification Trap

What is DSS Form W-1348 ("Proof We Need") & Why Does It Cause Denials?

When you apply for Connecticut Title 19, the Department of Social Services does not simply approve or deny your case. Instead, the caseworker reviews your initial disclosures and issues Form W-1348 ("Verification We Need").

This notice typically contains a list of 10 to 30 highly specific items: 60 months of missing bank statements, explanations for checks over $5,000, life insurance cash surrender values, or title documents.

The danger is the 10-day rule. By state statute, you have exactly 10 calendar days from the date printed on the letter (which often arrives in your mailbox on day 5 or 6!) to produce the records. If you fail to comply or request a formal extension, DSS automatically closes and denies your file.

Consequences of Missing W-1348

Standard CT DSS procedural outcome
  • Automatic Case Denial: The application is closed for "Failure to Provide Verification," not for financial ineligibility.
  • Loss of Retroactive Coverage: You lose up to 3 months of retroactive eligibility, leaving tens of thousands of unpaid nursing home debt.
  • Facility Discharge Threat: Without "Medicaid Pending" status, facilities issue formal 30-day discharge notices for non-payment.

The 4 Reasons Connecticut Cases Get Trapped

If your Medicaid application has been pending for 90, 120, or even 180 days with no answers, your file has likely fallen into one of these common administrative roadblocks.

Caseworker Turnover

DSS eligibility workers manage hundreds of files. When workers transfer, retire, or get reassigned between processing centers (Hartford, Bridgeport, Waterbury), pending files sit unreviewed for months.

File lost in regional routing queue

ConneCT Upload Glitches

Families upload documents through the state's ConneCT digital portal or fax to the DSS Scanning Center. If barcode routing sheets are omitted, files are misplaced or disconnected from your case number.

Unindexed document pileup

Unreconciled Bank Checks

If an older bank statement shows a check for $4,500 written 3 years ago without an image or receipt, the state caseworker marks the file as "Incomplete Transfer Audit" and freezes processing.

Missing transaction paper trail

Lack of Case Escalation

Calling the standard DSS 1-855 telephone customer service line rarely reaches the specific caseworker assigned to your file. Without knowing internal supervisory tiers, families wait helplessly.
