The Eldercare Dilemma

Why Connecticut Families Face Devastating Private-Pay Bills.

When an aging parent or spouse suffers a fall, stroke, or cognitive decline, hospital discharge planners frequently recommend immediate placement into a skilled nursing facility. Most families mistakenly believe that Medicare will pay for their ongoing care.

In reality, Medicare only covers short-term skilled rehabilitation—up to 100 days maximum, with heavy daily copayments starting on day 21. Once rehabilitation ends, care transitions to custodial nursing care, which Medicare does not cover.

Without Connecticut Title 19 (Medicaid / HUSKY C), families are thrust into private-pay status, receiving monthly nursing home invoices exceeding $16,000 that can wipe out a lifetime of family savings in under a year.

Statutory Requirements

The 3 Pillars of Connecticut Medicaid Eligibility

To qualify for Connecticut Title 19 (HUSKY C) long-term care benefits, an applicant must satisfy three distinct tests established in the Connecticut Department of Social Services Uniform Policy Manual (UPM).

01

Clinical Level of Care

The applicant must clinically require skilled nursing facility care or substantial hands-on assistance with multiple Activities of Daily Living (ADLs).

  • Bathing, dressing, and personal hygiene.
  • Mobility, transferring, and ambulation.
  • Cognitive impairment (Alzheimer's or dementia supervision).
Evaluated via Ascend / Maximus clinical screens.
02

Asset & Resource Limit

Connecticut imposes strict limits on "countable" liquid assets that an applicant may retain on the first day of the approval month.

  • Single Individual: Exactly $1,600 maximum in countable assets.
  • Community Spouse: Protected under CSRA limits (up to $154,140+ in 2024).
  • Exempt Assets: Primary home (if spouse resides there), 1 vehicle, prepaid burial.
Requires forensic pre-audit to avoid early denial.
03

Income & Patient Liability

Most monthly income does not disqualify an applicant, but it must be calculated and applied toward the nursing facility bill each month.

  • Personal Needs Allowance: $75/mo retained for clothing and personal care.
  • Spousal Income Diversion: Monthly income shifted to community spouse under MMMNA.
  • Applied Income: Balance paid directly to nursing facility.
Determined by strict DSS budgeting formula.
Crucial Compliance Rule

The Dreaded 60-Month Lookback Period

Connecticut DSS examines every single financial transaction across the 60 months preceding the Medicaid application date. Any uncompensated transfer or gift triggers a harsh penalty period during which Medicaid refuses to pay.

How DSS Calculates Penalty Periods

Statewide Average Monthly Nursing Home Rate (Penalty Divisor)

Under Connecticut statutes, if an applicant gifted money, transferred real estate for less than fair market value, or wrote checks to grandchildren within the 5-year window, DSS totals the transfers and divides by the state's average monthly nursing home cost (currently over $14,000/month). The resulting number is the exact number of months the senior must pay privately out of pocket before Medicaid starts.

Common Lookback Pitfalls

Cash withdrawals without receipts, loan repayments to relatives without formal promissory notes, selling a vehicle or boat below blue-book value, and quitclaim deeds executed without an elder law exemption.

Financial Protection

Permitted Spend-Down vs. Disallowed Gifting

You do not have to lose everything to qualify for Medicaid. Connecticut law allows strategic, lawful spend-down to benefit the senior and protect their spouse.

Permitted Spend-Down Strategies

Fully compliant under CT DSS Uniform Policy Manual
  • Irrevocable Prepaid Funeral Contracts: Up to the Connecticut statutory cap (currently $10,000 per spouse), plus burial space items.
  • Paying Off Legitimate Liabilities: Retiring mortgages, credit card balances, outstanding medical debts, or auto loans.
  • Primary Home Repairs & Capital Improvements: Roof replacements, heating systems, and safety modifications on the primary residence.
  • Purchasing Exempt Personal Property: A reliable vehicle for the community spouse, assistive medical equipment, or eyeglasses/dental care.

Disallowed Moves & Penalty Triggers

Triggers severe DSS penalty periods
  • Cash Gifts to Family Members: Transferring money to children or grandchildren, even under annual IRS gift tax exclusions ($18,000).
  • Deeding Real Estate for $1: Transferring property to children without qualifying under the statutory Caregiver Child Exemption.
  • Unrecorded Caregiver Payments: Paying a relative for home care without a formal, written personal service agreement and W-2 payroll trail.
  • Unexplained Large Cash Withdrawals: Withdrawing funds without paper receipts proving goods or services were received at market value.
