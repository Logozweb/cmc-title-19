Can I Own a Home and Qualify for Medicaid in Connecticut?

One of the most persistent myths is that a family must sell their house before applying for Medicaid. In Connecticut, owning a home does not automatically disqualify an applicant for Title 19—but home equity limits, spousal protections, and estate recovery liens play critical roles.

Common Questions

Connecticut Medicaid & Title 19 FAQs

Straightforward answers to the questions Connecticut families ask most.

What is the difference between Title 19, Medicaid, and Medicare?

Medicare is a federal health insurance program primarily for adults 65+ and certain younger individuals with disabilities, covering hospitalizations and short-term skilled rehab. Title XIX (Title 19) of the Social Security Act established Medicaid, which in Connecticut covers long-term nursing home care and home-care waivers. Unlike Medicare, Medicaid requires financial eligibility review.

What is Connecticut's 5-Year Lookback Period?

When an applicant applies for Connecticut Medicaid for Long-Term Services & Supports (LTSS), DSS reviews financial records and asset transfers covering the 60 months (5 years) preceding the application date. Any uncompensated transfers or gifts during this window may trigger a penalty period during which Medicaid will not pay for long-term care.

Can Compass help if my application is already stuck with DSS?

Yes. Many families come to Compass after an application is already pending, after receiving a DSS W-1348 verification request, or when communication with caseworkers has stalled. We audit the record, organize outstanding documents, and assist in responding within DSS deadlines.

When should a family hire an Elder Law Attorney vs. Compass Title19?

Compass provides non-attorney administrative Medicaid consulting, documentation review, and care navigation. When a family requires legal advice, drafting of irrevocable trusts, wills, deeds, special needs trusts, powers of attorney, or representation in contested administrative fair hearings, an elder law attorney should be consulted. We frequently work cooperatively alongside elder law attorneys.
